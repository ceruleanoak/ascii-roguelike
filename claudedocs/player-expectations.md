# Player Expectations — what the player brings in

A director's read of the loop: which conventions the player carries into it,
and what fills the silence where none applies. Companion to
`zone-cosmology.md`. Lines marked **(author)** are ratified; the rest are
proposals.

---

## 1. The game cannot tolerate perfectionism (author)

The one critical expectation. A perfectionist reads a run as an exam and a
death as a failed grade; both readings break the loop.

- **Death is progression, not failure.** PRESS SPACE TO CONTINUE does this
  work; TRY AGAIN would accuse. No death or reset text implies failure.
- **The speedrunner** is the perfectionist who has made peace with the reset.
- **Avoid meta-awareness.** The game shapes expectation by behaving, never by
  commenting.

## 2. Everything is interpretation (author)

A letter is an invitation to interpret intent. There are no wrong answers for
the player, who changes their own mind over time — and is spoiled, or proven
"wrong," by a wiki.

- A `d` does not read as "Duelist." It reads as a d, which you discover has a
  PARRY.
- `C` is just an exit in a unique font that happens never to hold enemies, and
  on exploration holds a campfire, a companion and a storyteller.
- Exit letters are set apart by font. Keyboard keys are something else
  entirely.

**The real collision is enemy vs. ingredient** — both plain letters on the
field, and magnetism makes a drop sliding toward the player read as an enemy
closing in. Direction (author): a slow "pick me up" blink, starting before the
magnet delay wears off. Its rhythm must stay distinct from a Telegraph blink.

## 3. Frames (author)

- **Arcade is the anchor** — no saves, free resets, the roster reads as lives.
  The only contract the game honors in full.
- Random rooms should read as **Zelda-inspired roguelike**, not Zelda
  conventions.
- Roguelites lean on familiarity in the loop to show progression. **Same
  impression, different reason**: familiar because the player learned, not
  because something unlocked.
- **Dungeons are the biggest risk.** Self-contained, with very few in-dungeon
  unlocks — where the Zelda contract (the dungeon gives its item, the item
  stays yours) creeps back.
- **Puzzle rooms are teaching moments, with the gift of the tool** — the
  Compass Rose hands over the Compass.

## 4. Minibosses are proving grounds (author)

Unprepared, it doesn't go well.

| Miniboss | Punishes |
|---|---|
| Slime boss | Reaching 3 slime pips |
| Centipede | Not adjusting playstyle to its range |
| Sniper | Not dodge rolling |

These deaths above all must read as *now I know what it wants*.

## 5. Silence and its signatures

- **The avatar answers for itself (author).** A flash and/or screen shake for
  "something is broken"; a yellow `?` above the player's glyph for "what you
  did didn't work or make sense." The avatar's agency is alluded to by the
  Cursed Run and hardly developed. (The Compass's Passive Indicator moved to
  `!` to free `?`.)
- **Spells take ENTER (author)** — explicit nowhere, learned through FROG and
  HEX and maybe future Wise Fellow scripts. SPACE and Shift also submit the
  buffer (`main.js` keydown), but each fires its own action too; Enter is the
  only clean submit.
- **The THREE's voice oversteps (author).** `UNKNOWN SPELL.` is the example.
  Silence says more; words that fill empty air spend the silence that was
  doing the teaching.
- **Reserve the corruption aesthetic for the Infinite Loop.** It must read as a
  glitch, so a real tofu glyph or garbage frame in normal play is a false
  sighting (rulebook G3).

## 6. Sell the secret (author)

You can't fight the wiki or the speedrun. Players long to recreate the feeling
of the first Great Plateau in *Breath of the Wild* — but once you spend it,
it's gone. So it is with Pure Rogue. The secret is an experience, not
information: make the first discovery whole enough that whoever looked it up
knows what they traded away.
